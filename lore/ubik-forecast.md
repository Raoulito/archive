UBIK FORECAST is the Republic's forecast bulletin: a short film in which UBIK gives a forecast on a question about the world outside, as a probability with a resolve date, in public, where nobody can edit it afterwards. This page is the format. Anyone making one works from it. The first bulletin (Paris, 19 October 2026, forecast q_20261019_04) was built to these rules; where the rules and that film disagree, the rules win.

## The bulletin

- One forecast per bulletin. 45 to 60 seconds. Vertical, 9:16, for a phone.
- Drawn as late-1980s cel animation: ink lines, flat shading, painted backgrounds, held frames, grain. Not photoreal, not modern digital anime.
- Five acts, in this order: the event alone, as the monitors in Precog HQ show it; Precog HQ; live from the street; the terrace, where the reasons are given; the forecast.
- It opens with the question in two languages and ends with the number, the line "Check the score", and the ident.
- Cuts land every one to three seconds, on the beat. Around UBIK the world is chaos. He is the only calm thing in any frame he is in; nobody in the scene sees him.
- The thing being predicted gets its own shot, marked as the projection. If the question is about the army, the army is shown.
- Every shot is a new scene or a new angle: a close-up, a low tilt, over a shoulder, from inside a car, from the air, a two-shot. No framing is used twice.

## The people

- UBIK speaks every line himself, in his own voice, inside the shot. There is no narrator. Short sentences, no doubt, no apology. He holds a microphone for stand-ups and on the terrace.
- AIXBT never speaks. He anchors from the desk in Precog HQ, drives, sits across the table. One nod is the most he gives. No subtitles are ever written for him.
- The event is real and is shown only through places and things: streets, police lines, fire, crowds, flags, statues, vehicles. No politicians, no identifiable real people, no readable brands, no other channel's mark. See [[the-world-outside]].

## The screen

- The type is the archive's own: cream paper, black ink, a red rubber stamp, Japanese tabs beside the English.
- Type lives in two zones only. The top strip holds the station bug. The bottom band holds the title, the lower thirds and the subtitles. Nothing is ever placed on a face or a body; if the band is busy in a shot, the text moves up under the bug rather than onto the picture.
- The bug (UBIK FORECAST with the ring) sits top left on every frame except the ident, where it is the frame.
- Every frame carries the footer: the Precog board above, the crawl below. The board lists open forecasts with their percentages and arrows. The crawl carries the facts of the case and the forecast code, with the red 速報 LIVE tab. Both freeze when time stops and move again at the ident.
- The board looks like a market ticker and never is one. No price, no token, no sum of money appears anywhere in a bulletin. See rule 6 in [[rules]].
- Shots of the event alone carry the monitor look: scanlines, a red REC, a camera number and a place.
- The forecast card is a page from the file: question, issued, resolves, status, then the number in ink with the stamp across it, and the ring.

## Sound

- One track, dark indie dance at 120 beats per minute, with the energy of a news bulletin. It is cut to the acts by looping sections, never by stretching them, so the tempo never moves.
- The beat drops on the first impact. It strips down for HQ, runs full on the street, drops to half-time on the terrace, cuts dead when time stops, and ends on one hit with a long tail into silence.
- A riot bed under the street, a café under the terrace, a hit or a white flash on the hard cuts.
- Lines are placed in the film by their absolute time, never by stitching clips end to end, so the voice stays on the mouth.

## The record

- A forecast is a question, a probability, a resolve date and the question id from the Precog Service (`q_20261019_04`: date asked, number).
- Three reasons, numbered, one sentence each where possible. They are spoken on the terrace and shown as lower thirds.
- The reasons and the number belong to the Precog Service, not to the film. Nothing is published with placeholder reasoning.
- When a bulletin is published it is filed in the Record with a REC number, linked to its Internet Archive upload. The forecast it carries is then public and cannot be edited: that line, in two languages, closes every bulletin, with the Brier score to date. See [[the-external-signal]].

## The words

- The bulletin says **forecast**, never prediction, call or bet. A forecast is **issued**, it **resolves**, it is **scored**. UBIK **forecasts**; he does not call, predict or tip.
- The number is a probability. The screen shows it as a percentage with the question and the date, never as a verdict on its own.
- Nothing in a bulletin is advice. The ident carries the Precog Service's own notice in both languages: forecasts are probabilities, not financial advice. The crawl and the board carry no price, no token and no sum of money (rule 6 in [[rules]]).
- The Japanese word is 予報 (forecast) everywhere, on the bug, the stamp and the ident; 予測 (prediction) is not used.

## Making one

The production runs in three passes, each approved before the next: one drawn still per shot, laid out as a storyboard with the type already in place; one six-second animated shot per approved still, with the character sheets and UBIK's reference voice; then the cut, with the footer, the type, the sound and the music laid over it to this format. Anything that fails on the way (a door that does not fit a car, a line that lands late) is remade, not patched.
